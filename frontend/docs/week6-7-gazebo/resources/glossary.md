# Glossary: Robot Simulation with Gazebo

## A

**Actuator**: A device that converts control signals into physical motion in the robot, such as motors for joints.

**API (Application Programming Interface)**: A set of rules and protocols for building and interacting with software applications, such as ROS 2 services and topics.

## C

**Collision Detection**: The computational process of detecting when two or more bodies come into contact with each other in the simulation.

**Contact Stabilization**: Methods used to maintain stable contact between objects during simulation, preventing jittering or unrealistic bouncing.

## F

**Friction**: The force that opposes the relative motion or tendency of such motion of two surfaces in contact, critical for humanoid robot stability.

**Forward Kinematics**: The process of calculating the position and orientation of the end-effector based on the joint angles.

## G

**Gazebo**: A physics-based simulation environment that allows users to accurately and efficiently develop and test robots for real-world applications.

**Gravity**: The force that attracts a body toward the center of the earth, typically set to 9.8 m/s² in simulations for realistic behavior.

## I

**Inverse Kinematics**: The process of calculating the joint angles required to achieve a desired end-effector position and orientation.

**Inertial Properties**: Physical properties of a body that determine its resistance to changes in motion, including mass and moment of inertia.

## J

**Joint**: A connection between two links that allows relative motion between them, such as revolute (rotational) or prismatic (linear) joints.

## L

**LiDAR (Light Detection and Ranging)**: A sensing method that uses laser light to measure distances and create 3D maps of the environment.

## P

**Physics Engine**: The software component that simulates the laws of physics, including gravity, collisions, and material properties.

**Plugin**: A modular software component that adds specific functionality to Gazebo, such as sensors or controllers.

## R

**ROS (Robot Operating System)**: A flexible framework for writing robot software that provides services designed for a heterogeneous computer cluster.

**ROS 2**: The second generation of the Robot Operating System with improved features for real-time and embedded systems.

## S

**SDF (Simulation Description Format)**: The native XML-based format for describing objects and environments in Gazebo simulation.

**Sensor**: A device that detects or measures a physical property and records, indicates, or otherwise responds to it, such as cameras, IMUs, or LiDAR.

**Simulation**: The imitation of the operation of a real-world process or system over time, particularly important for testing humanoid robots safely.

## U

**URDF (Unified Robot Description Format)**: An XML format for representing a robot model, including kinematic and visual properties, commonly used with ROS.

## V

**Virtual Reality**: A simulated experience that can be similar to or completely different from the real world, sometimes used in conjunction with robot simulation.
